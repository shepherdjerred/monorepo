# Red Warfare Search and Destroy kits

`domain/kit/KitBook.java` lists 51 kit definitions in menu order: Red Warfare's
twenty-two playable kits in the order of the original `SearchAndDestroy`, then
Equality, Inertia and Sacrificial, which the original never offered in play,
then The Storm's first eight designed kits and the eighteen kits in
`domain/kit/ExpansionKits.java`. TSMC's ordinary match pool in `rwf.yml` admits
only the 24 ported kits: the original twenty-two plus Equality and Sacrificial.
Inertia and the Storm-designed kits remain available to development and test
profiles without entering ordinary TSMC matches.

The ability of each kit that has one is a pure class under `domain/ability/kits`,
registered in `AbilityBook` and driven by match events with randomness from the
match seed.
`rwf/kits.yml` must describe the same kit definitions, kit for kit.

Every kit carries the gold "Bomb Fuse" in hotbar slot 0. A kit's fuse bonus
(`FuseType`) takes a second per level off arming, defusing or both. A bow kit's
last arrow sits in inventory slot 9, off the hotbar, as Red Warfare kept it.

Six kits are free for good: Trooper, Longbow, Shortbow, Rewind, Medic and
Teleporter. Every other kit is bought once with Red Warfare credits, and four
bought kits rotate free each ISO week. A kit's tier follows its price: up to
200 credits is basic, up to 350 mid, and more is advanced. The free kits are
basic (Trooper, Longbow, Shortbow) or mid (Rewind, Medic, Teleporter). While
the managed flag `the-storm-rwf-kits-enabled` is off for a player, only the
first four kits are offered to them: Trooper, Longbow, Shortbow and Rewind.

Armour is listed boots, leggings, chestplate, helmet. "Archer armour" is
chainmail boots, iron leggings, iron chestplate and a chainmail helmet.

## Native weapons and combat

Sword and axe kits keep Red Warfare's damage formula and attack-speed modifier.
Spears, tridents and maces use the native weapon operation and its cooldown;
equipping one or swapping it into the main hand removes the legacy modifier.
Changing back restores it. Crossbows have separate charge, load and fire actions;
bows, crossbows and tridents use the actual selected item and ammunition for
humans and Citizens. Finite ammunition, Multishot, Piercing, tipped and spectral
arrows, returning Loyalty tridents, spear contacts and mace landing resets stay
with the native server operation.

`ItemSpec.potions` is required content, including an empty list on ordinary
items. Each payload names its effect, amplifier and delivered duration in ticks.
Authored tipped arrows set the supported potion duration scale to one, so
Fletcher's 60-tick Slowness lasts three seconds. Alchemist flasks apply an exact
three-block, enemy-only splash through their ability rules. Weakness reduces
legacy melee raw damage by four per level; modern raw damage already includes it.

Modern hits complete their native damage event with one authoritative RWF
amount. `PaperNativeDamage` binds the pinned Paper modifier API at startup
because Paper has no supported final-damage setter. It preserves shield blocks
and native absorption consumption while preventing armour from applying twice.
`PaperNativeItems` similarly binds the exact native use, release and spear-jab
operations missing from the public API. A linkage mismatch fails startup.

The combat tracker owns the shared hit window: in the first ten ticks after a
hit, an equal or weaker hit is blocked and a stronger hit deals only its extra
damage. Native invulnerability is zero during RWF so it cannot filter a hit
before these rules. Player snapshots restore the previous native maximum when
leaving or recovering after a crash; historical snapshots restore twenty ticks.

### Decided for The Storm

- Equality, Inertia and Sacrificial were never on sale in Red Warfare
  (Equality was owner-only, Sacrificial staff-only, and Inertia's ability an
  empty stub). Here they can be bought like any other kit, and prices and the
  currency use the original credits.
- On the designed kits: the Bannerman's banner heals allies around it, not the
  Bannerman itself; the Tracker's marks show on its team's compasses, so only
  teammates holding a compass see them (no kit carries one); and the Spotter's
  spyglass keeps its vanilla zoom, marking on use.

### Deviations from Red Warfare

Every ported kit and killstreak follows libraryaddict's code. What remains below
is only what Minecraft 26.2 or Paper no longer offers, with the closest
equivalent used instead.

- The Juggernaut's client was kept from sprinting by ProtocolLib rewriting the
  food level in every health packet to 6. Paper cannot rewrite packets, so the
  same 6-food health update is sent every tick instead.
- Red Warfare's safe-landing check read a hand-written table of Minecraft
  1.12's blocks. Blocks added since have no entry, so each block is classed by
  the rule the table followed (see `adapter/paper/Footings.java`).
- Kit items keep Red Warfare's item flags except 1.12's `HIDE_POTION_EFFECTS`,
  whose successor `HIDE_ADDITIONAL_TOOLTIP` Paper has deprecated; on these
  kits' items it hid nothing. The flags added since (dye, armour trim, stored
  enchantments) stay off as 1.12 could not set them. The Venom sword's glint
  came from a registered custom enchantment, which 26.2 cannot register; it
  uses the enchantment glint override instead.
- Frost's ice burst was the `STEP_SOUND` effect, which Paper has marked for
  removal; the same crumbs and break sound the client drew for it are sent
  directly.
- The Bleeder's dark red drops relied on the 1.12 client wrapping a negative
  dust colour into dark red; they are drawn as dark red dust directly.
- Red Warfare hid parts of a player with packets (ProtocolLib and LibsDisguises), which
  Paper's API cannot do per viewer. The Skinner's worn skin is its profile texture, so its
  own team sees the skin too (with its own name tag); the Ghost's and Wraith's footsteps are
  not silenced; an RPG is a hidden arrow with an egg display riding along, in place of an
  arrow disguised as an egg. The worn name is a display over the Skinner's hidden name tag,
  so the health enemy Medics' boards showed under that name is a second display line, shown
  to those Medics, in place of a below-name score.
- Red Warfare's `UtilBlock` listed which 1.12 blocks were full solid cubes. Teleporter
  placement reads a modern block's material instead: opaque full cubes plus glass, leaves,
  ice and the other see-through cubes it listed are solid, and anything without collision is
  open.
- Killstreaks, where Minecraft 26.2 no longer has what 1.12 had:
  - Minecraft 1.12 had one spawn egg item, told apart by its data; the attack
    dogs and the iron golem come as the wolf and iron golem spawn eggs.
  - Red Warfare's crate labels were per-viewer packet armour stands; they are
    per-viewer text displays at the same height.
  - Wolves have coats and voices now; attack dogs get the pale coat and classic
    voice wolves had in 1.12. An angry wolf's anger now runs out after 20 to
    39 s, where 1.12 kept it until cleared; their hunting does not depend on
    it.
  - The golem's goals are Paper mob goals in place of reflected 1.12 pathfinder
    goals. The server weighs which goals to start every 2 ticks, where 1.12
    did every 3, and a stroll takes the first spot over a whole block as 1.12
    did, with the server's own pathfinding.
  - Napalm's fireballs smoke with the particle 1.12 called `explode`, now
    `poof`.

## Buying, the rotation and layouts

The menu, lobby alcove displays, new purchases and weekly rotation include only
the current draft's kit pool. A pool that cannot fill four slots with the
required tiers and no repeats has no weekly free rotation. A charged pending
purchase can still settle after a draft change excludes its kit.

- `/rwf buy <kit>` or the kit menu's confirmation buys a kit with Red Warfare
  credits (3 for a win, 1 for a loss). A purchase is written as a pending row
  in `rwf_kit_purchase` before the credits move, so a crash between charging
  and granting is settled on the next start; owned kits live in
  `rwf_kit_owned`.
- `domain/kit/KitRotation.java` picks four bought kits each ISO week (Monday
  00:00 UTC): one or two basic, the rest mid or advanced, none free the week
  before, from a fixed seed. Nothing is scheduled: the week computes it.
- A right-click on a usable kit in the kit menu opens the layout editor. It
  closes saving where each item ended up, the fuse locked in slot 0, as the
  player's layout for that kit in `rwf_player_prefs.kit_layouts`. A saved
  layout that no longer fits the kit falls back to the kit's own.
- The kit a player last played (`rwf_player_prefs.last_kit`) is picked for them
  again when they join, if they may still pick it.

## Killstreaks

Killstreak rewards sit behind the per-player managed flag
`the-storm-rwf-killstreaks-enabled`, ported from Red Warfare's
`KillstreakManager` and `StreakBase`. A fighter's killstreak counts a kill as 1
and an assist as its share of the damage. Each time the running total passes a
whole number, the rewards for that count are given:

| Reward           | Kills     | What it does                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---------------- | --------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Tracking compass | exactly 2 | A compass that points at the nearest living enemy                                                                                                                                   |
| Attack dogs      | every 4   | Three wolves (14 health, bites of 6) that hunt enemies in sight within 14 blocks                                                                                                    |
| Supply crate     | every 4   | A chest the owner loots at once, teammates after 30 s and anyone after 60 s: 1–3 steak, 1–3 ender pearls (none in the endgame), and half the time an unbreakable diamond chestplate |
| Iron golem       | every 8   | An iron golem (40 health, landing with 20) that hits for 13–14 and throws its victim up                                                                                             |
| Napalm           | every 11  | Sets enemies within 20 blocks alight, then a power-9 blast hurts everyone off the thrower's team                                                                                    |

Each reward arrives as an item, in a Ghost's or Wraith's second empty slot
(the first is the empty hand that hides it, and with no second they get
nothing), or added to anyone else's inventory. As in Red Warfare, a fighter's
item is known by its material alone, so any compass tracks and any TNT is
napalm, and either click uses it.

- The compass points at the nearest living enemy, the last of equally near ones
  in a shuffled order. A Ghost or Wraith is placed up to 8 blocks off, a stray
  that steps by -1 or 0 each half second on each axis and so sinks to -8. The
  found enemy hears an experience orb (volume 4, pitch 0) and a message at
  most every 30 s; the tracker learns the distance at most every 2 s.
- Every other item is used on a block: the space against the clicked face must
  be air on a whole solid block (and a crate keeps a block clear of other
  crates and off a bomb's column, saying why). One item of that material is
  taken, off hand first. A falling block then drops from about 100 blocks
  above it, never less than 50 above the first air over the column, and booms
  where it lands (pitch 7). All but napalm settle onto the nearest spot one
  can walk through, within ten blocks up or down.
- Supply crates face north three times in four, as Red Warfare's random block
  data did, and are labelled for the owner, their team and everyone else, each
  counting down to when they may loot. A looter already carrying a diamond
  chestplate, worn or not, gets no second one.
- Attack dogs are tamed by their owner, in the team's collar colour and angry.
  Each second a dog keeps prey it may still have, or takes the nearest
  creature in sight within 14 blocks that is not of its team (a Ghost or
  Wraith only once it sprints, holds something or has an arrow in it), and
  runs back at speed 1 (1.4 when its owner sprints) once more than 1.5 blocks
  (16 while it hunts) from a spot 3 blocks ahead of its owner, giving up its
  prey. They go when their owner dies.
- The iron golem thinks with Red Warfare's goals (`GolemAi`): it swims, chases
  its target and hits it in reach every 20 ticks (30 after a blow that lands),
  strolls, and looks at players within 8 blocks or around. It takes as target
  the nearest player it sees within 16 blocks across (less for one sneaking or
  invisible) that Red Warfare's selector allows, else the nearest mob within
  16 across and 4 up or down; it never takes a creeper.
- Napalm warns everyone within 40 blocks of the thrower as it falls ("Napalm
  has been unleased in the area by …", in the original's spelling), driven down
  at 0.6 blocks a tick and hissing. Where it lands, every creature off the
  thrower's team within 20 blocks burns for 5 ticks less per block away; Fire
  Protection leaves `min(80, 15 × levels)` per cent of that, as Red Warfare
  reckoned it. That fire is napalm's, through armour, credited to the thrower
  while it burns. A power-9 blast then hurts every creature off the team, and
  a ring of fireballs sets the ground alight where they stop.
- A summon's blow is a melee hit credited to its owner, through the hit window,
  the victim's armour and kit: a dog's bite of 6 knocks back as any melee hit,
  the golem's 13 or 14 throws its victim straight up. A fighter's hit on an
  enemy summon goes through the same rules. Summons never hurt or target their
  own team, leave the dead alone, and are removed when the match resets.
